What to do in the first 48 hours after a storm

A calm sequence protects your home and your claim. These are the same steps we walk callers through.

  1. 1

    Stay safe and stop more damage

    Keep off a wet or damaged roof. Move belongings away from leaks and cover openings with a tarp if you can do it safely from inside or the ground.

  2. 2

    Photograph everything

    Take dated photos of the roof from the ground, damaged gutters, dented vents, ceiling stains and the debris in your yard.

  3. 3

    Read your policy

    Find your wind and hail deductible and your claim deadline. Ask your insurer what documentation it requires.

  4. 4

    Get a documented inspection

    A written, photo-backed inspection tells you whether to file. Compare more than one opinion if the estimate is large.

  5. 5

    Choose a contractor carefully

    Get a written estimate, proof of insurance, references and a permit. Never sign a contract that assigns your claim.

Who are you, and do you do the repairs?+

We are an independent referral service. You call us, we ask a few questions about your damage and location, and we connect you with a roofing or restoration company that serves your area. The contractor does the work, and you decide whether to hire them.

Does it cost anything to call?+

No. Calling and getting matched is free for homeowners. We may be paid by the contractor when a qualified call is connected, and that does not change your price or obligate you to hire anyone.

How do I know if hail damaged my roof?+

Look for dark spots where granules are missing, soft or bruised shingles, granules in the gutters and dents on vents, flashing or gutters. The reliable way is a documented roof inspection after the storm.

How long do I have to file a storm damage claim in Texas?+

It depends on your policy. Many policies set a filing deadline, often one year from the date of loss, and some are shorter. Check your declarations page or ask your agent now rather than later.

Can a roofer waive my insurance deductible?+

No. Texas law prohibits contractors from waiving or rebating your deductible, so treat any such offer as a warning sign.

Should I sign with the first roofer who knocks on my door after a storm?+

Take your time. Storm chasers from out of state often arrive after big hail events. Ask for a Texas address, insurance proof, references and a written estimate, and do not sign a contract that assigns your insurance claim.
